A.L.F.R.E.D.\ sits at the intersection of four threads: the SLM-as-agent thesis, skill memory and self-evolving agents, tool-use LLMs, and agent routing.

\textbf{The SLM thesis.} Belcak et al.~\cite{belcak2025slm} argue that small language models are sufficiently powerful and architecturally better suited for agentic systems, since agent invocations are typically narrow, repetitive, and bounded. A.L.F.R.E.D.\ is an empirical realisation of that claim: by routing repeatable intents to a small Reflexer, we trade general reasoning for the cost profile the position paper describes.

\textbf{Tool-use LLMs and the SLM tier.} Toolformer~\cite{schick2023toolformer} and Gorilla~\cite{patil2023gorilla} embed tool selection in model weights via fine-tuning. TinyAgent~\cite{erdogan2024tinyagent} pushes the same setting on-device, showing a 1.1 to 7B fine-tuned model can surpass GPT-4-Turbo on a curated macOS-API assistant benchmark, which is direct empirical support for our choice of a 3B Reflexer. A.L.F.R.E.D.\ is middleware: no weight changes, the same backbone pairs with different skill catalogues, and the skill registry is one JSON edit away.

\textbf{Procedural memory and the nearest neighbour.} Memp~\cite{fang2025memp} distils past trajectories into both step-by-step instructions and higher-level script abstractions. A.L.F.R.E.D.'s patterns are Memp's script abstractions compiled into deterministic templates with slot schemas and validators. Memento~\cite{zhou2025memento} is the case-based, non-parametric foundation; Memento-Skills~\cite{zhou2026mementoskills} extends it with a learned skill router over markdown skills and a Read-Write Reflective Learning loop. Memento-Skills is the closest published neighbour: the core distinction is that their router is a behaviour-trainable neural component, while ours is a feature-engineered regex+Laplace scorer running in microseconds, and we route to a small local Reflexer rather than a single skill-conditioned executor.

\textbf{Routing, cascading, reasoning.} ReAct~\cite{yao2022react} is what our Thinker tier \emph{is}, retained as the unrouted fallback. Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} is a self-correction loop applied after failure; we explicitly disambiguate our cheap-path \textbf{Reflexer} from Reflexion in \S\ref{sec:system}. RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} routes \emph{between models} based on query difficulty; we route at the \emph{intent} level. Dekoninck et al.~\cite{dekoninck2024unifiedrouting} unify routing and cascading; A.L.F.R.E.D.'s two-tier path is a structured cascade in that framing, with the additional property that the Reflexer tier runs a small local model.

\textbf{Benchmarks and alternative paths.} AgentBench~\cite{liu2023agentbench} and AssistantBench~\cite{yoran2024assistantbench} establish the multi-task and realistic-assistant evaluation patterns adopted by our 53-task suite. A weight-level alternative is to update model parameters from live conversation~\cite{wang2026openclawrl}; we chose non-parametric compilation to make skills auditable, portable across backbones, and free of catastrophic-forgetting risk.
